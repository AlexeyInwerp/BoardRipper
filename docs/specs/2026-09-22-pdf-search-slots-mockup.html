PDF search: a typed search and a board lookup, side by side

You search a PDF for 10UF 25V and walk its twelve hits. You double-click a capacitor on the board to see where it sits in the schematic. Today that second action erases the first: the field now says C7301, the twelve hits are gone, and the position in them with it.

2026-09-22 · mockup for the search-slots feature · companion to the report in this session

What goes wrong today

  1. One field, two writers. Your typing and the board's double-click write the same searchQuery. triggerFollowPdf(part, force=true) is the double-click path; force is what lets it overwrite a search you typed. A single click already defers to a typed search — only the double-click does not.
  2. Nothing remembers where you were. There is no search history anywhere in the app, and activeMatchIndex lives on the document, so a new query resets it. "3 of 12" cannot be recovered without retyping and stepping.
  3. "In view" is too generous. At 300 % zoom and up, a hit with any pixel inside the viewport counts as visible, so the viewer only blinks it and does not move. A hit half off the right edge stays half off the right edge.

Proposal

Inside the dotted band, or move

A hit counts as visible only when its whole box lies inside the viewport minus a 10 % margin (the dotted band). Otherwise the view centres on it. The move goes through syncTransform → clampPan, so pan boundaries and single-page lock still hold; at a boundary the hit ends up as close to centre as the page allows.

The rules behind the pictures

ThingRule
Two slotsFind is what you typed; Lookup is what the board sent (double-click, ⌘F with a selection, PDF-follow). Each has its own query, hits and position. A lookup replaces the previous lookup and never Find. Clearing the field clears Find only; × on the chip clears Lookup only.
Active slotExactly one is active, marked by the accent underline. Enter, Shift+Enter, ↑ ↓, the ▲ ▼ buttons and the ⌘F repeat all step the active slot. Typing or clicking the field activates Find; clicking the chip activates Lookup. Activating a slot returns to its saved position, it does not restart at 1.
LockOpen (default): a new lookup becomes active and the view jumps to it — today's behaviour. Closed: a new lookup fills the chip, marks its hits, shows the fresh-dot, and leaves the view alone. The lock is per PDF panel and remembered. ⌘⇧L toggles it.
ColoursFind hits · Lookup hits · the active hit. These are the three fills the viewer already paints (PdfViewerPanel.tsx:2138–2153), so nothing new enters the palette; only the assignment changes.
HistoryPer PDF, last 20 typed searches, newest first, each with hit count and last position. Stored in localStorage under boardripper-pdf-search-history, keyed by file name. Opens on focus of an empty field or ↓ in it; Enter restores query and position; ⌫ on a row forgets it. The board's sidebar search gets the same list, keyed by board file.
RecentreOn step or on activation: if the hit's box is not entirely inside the viewport shrunk by 10 % on each side, centre on it at the current zoom (never zoom out; the existing 3× floor stays). Clamping is unchanged, so a locked page cannot be dragged past its edge to satisfy the rule.
Store shapePdfSearchSlot { query, matches, groups, activeIndex, source } twice on the document (find, lookup), plus activeSlot and lookupLock. searchText(q,'user') writes find; lookupEntity(...) writes lookup. The highlight painter reads both; the centring effect reads the active one.

Highlighting on the board is not drawn here because it needs a decision first: (a) keep the PDF hits marked persistently rather than only blinking, (b) outline the parts on the board whose refdes appears among the PDF hits, or (c) both. The pictures above assume (a) — every hit of both slots stays marked while the slot is filled.
